Development Deep Dive

Dynasty Archer is a Three Kingdoms action-roguelike for mobile, and over roughly two and a half years (390+ commits, 2022 to 2024) I became the person who carried its content pipeline. My work sat between design and engineering: I designed hero and boss behavior, then built it in Unity and wired it into the config data that drives each release.

The core of that work was heroes. I built or completed 18+ heroes end to end, from imported model and animation, prefab, active and passive skills, projectiles and VFX, to voice lines, icons, the hero select screen, try-hero mode, and shard config. Many skills needed their own logic: Liu Bei summons a Shadow Runner clone, Lu Bu drains life, and Xiahou Yuan uses a channeled cast. I also built the mount system as a second progression layer, with several UI iterations, mount data, 10+ mounts, per-hero riding models, and mount skills.

On the PvE side I finished chapters 1–18. Each chapter included its map layout, new monster AI, monster projectiles, a boss set, and config. I rebuilt the chapter 1–5 monsters on the new set, added an event boss and an endless map, and designed the Lu Bu World Boss, including its mechanics and damage balance. Around this I handled texture optimization, asset folder restructuring, and fixes for trails, colliders, hitboxes, melee range, and boss hit flicker. I also balanced heroes for PvP.

Releases were config-driven, so I updated chapter, hero, shard, and avatar config and toggled features per release, and maintained localization (including Japanese and skill names) as new content shipped.

Design Spotlight: Hero Skill Kits

Every hero had to feel different to play while still fitting one shared pipeline. I started from a skill archetype for each hero, built it on top of the shared skill and projectile systems, and only wrote custom logic where the archetype needed it.

Development Deep Dive

Heroes Survivor is a roguelike bullet-hell game for mobile, built in Unity with C# and PureMVC. From June 2023 to October 2024 I worked as a gameplay developer, and most of that time went into enemy and boss behavior, then into new heroes, skills, seasonal events, and performance work.

The largest area was boss and enemy AI. I created and finished 20+ bosses, each with its own AI, bullet patterns, and a precast system that warns the player before a big attack lands. Boss mechanics included self-healing, summoning minions, and cleaning up minions when the boss dies. I also reworked the bosses for chapters 1, 2, 3, and 5, along with many elites and creeps. In June 2023 alone I fixed behavior on dozens of mob and boss IDs: walk-through-wall bugs, attack range and speed, rotation, jump, dig, and rush movement.

For heroes I added new characters (Ares, Truci, Seraphina, Ankala, Mrs. Hazard, and others), built their skills and projectiles, and wrote skill evolutions for existing heroes and weapons. I also made the pet and NPC system and balanced the ultimates of the starter heroes. Seasonal events (Halloween, Thanksgiving, Noel and New Year, Lunar New Year) each shipped with their own boss, elite, creep, VFX, and config, along with a bullet-hell boss and its spawn logic.

A mobile bullet-hell game puts a lot of load on the device, so I spent a long stretch on optimization: smaller textures and meshes, removing unused meshes and sources, reducing draw calls, several VFX passes, and integrating Adaptive Performance. I also integrated Zendesk Support and Help Center, and kept the game data current by editing the character, weapon, buff, and wave config tables.

Development Deep Dive

On Samkok Heroes TD, my work was spread across two disciplines: game design and technical design. The mission was to build a Hero Tower Defense game with long campaign content and strong retention systems, and to keep that content balanced and shippable across live updates. Over roughly 16 months I made 500+ commits, and most of them were data and content work rather than engine code.

The largest body of work was the campaign itself. I built and balanced the Normal and Elite campaigns up to 90 chapters, with each chapter assembled from its map and layout, waves and formations, mobs, elites and bosses, rewards, and unlock rules. I also designed the Trial of Strength and Conquest modes, and supporting systems such as the Monopoly minigame, Rookie Quest, and the login reward calendar. Everything was driven through config data (over 550 config files touched), so a change could be tested and tuned without a code change.

Balancing was iterative: I measured, adjusted, and reverted when a change did not hold up. The clearest example is the rebalance of chapters 46–90, where I separated monster HP from wave density so the two could be tuned independently, smoothed the difficulty spike around chapters 46–48, and set a difficulty curve that carries through to chapter 90. I also reworked the chapter reward curve and fixed the gear tier calculation, and ran balance passes on heroes, classes, bosses, chips, and gems.

On the technical side I designed the skill unlock logic, built a new monster and boss AI set (including multi-phase bosses with their own combos, weapons, and projectiles).

Design Spotlight: Rebalancing Chapters 46–90

Problem

Late chapters were hard to tune. Monster HP and wave density were tied together, so every fix to one shifted the other, and difficulty spiked sharply around chapters 46–48.

Approach

I separated monster HP from wave density so each could be tuned on its own, then defined one difficulty curve running to chapter 90 for both Normal and Elite. Changes were measured, and reverted when they did not hold up.

Outcome

The spike around chapters 46–48 was smoothed, the curve carries through to chapter 90, and chapters can be retuned through config alone, with no code change. I also reworked the chapter reward curve to match.
